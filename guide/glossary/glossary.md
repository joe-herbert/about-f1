Quick definitions of the words you'll hear on a race weekend. Where there's a full page on the topic, the definition links to it.

## A

### Active aerodynamics
Movable front and rear wing elements that switch between a high-downforce setting for corners and a low-drag setting for straights. New for 2026. See [active aerodynamics](/guide/car/active-aerodynamics/).

### Aero rake
A frame of sensors fitted to the car in practice to measure airflow. See [practice sessions](/guide/basics/practice-sessions/).

### Apex
The point on the inside of a corner that a driver aims to clip. Late in the corner is a "late apex".

### Appeal
A team's challenge to a stewards' decision, heard by the FIA's International Court of Appeal. Some penalties, such as drive-throughs, can't be appealed. See [protests, appeals and the right of review](/guide/penalties/appeals/).

### ATR
Aerodynamic Testing Restrictions. The sliding scale that gives lower-placed teams more wind tunnel and CFD time. See [wind tunnel and CFD limits](/guide/teams/aero-testing-restrictions/).

## B

### Ballast
Weights added to bring a car up to the minimum weight or to change its balance. See [weight and dimensions](/guide/car/weight-and-dimensions/).

### Bargeboard
Bodywork beside the cockpit that guided airflow. Largely gone from current cars.

### Black and orange flag
The car has a mechanical problem, such as a loose part, and must pit to have it fixed. Also called the "meatball" flag.

### Black and white flag
A warning to a driver for unsporting behaviour, usually repeated track limits offences. See [black flags and warnings](/guide/flags/black-flags/).

### Black flag
The driver is disqualified and must return to the pits. See [black flags and warnings](/guide/flags/black-flags/).

### Blue flag
Shown to a lapped car to tell it to let the faster car behind through. See [blue flags and lapped cars](/guide/flags/blue-flags/).

### Boost
Extra electrical power a driver can use from the battery by pressing a button, to attack or defend. See [overtake mode and boost](/guide/car/overtake-mode/).

### Bottoming
When the floor or the plank underneath the car hits the track surface. It can damage the car and cause sparks.

### Box
Radio code for "come into the pits". "Box, box" means pit this lap.

### Brake balance
How braking force is split between the front and rear wheels. Drivers adjust it on the steering wheel, often several times a lap.

### Brake bias
Another name for brake balance.

## C

### CFD
Computational Fluid Dynamics. Computer simulation of airflow over the car, limited by the [aero testing rules](/guide/teams/aero-testing-restrictions/).

### Chequered flag
The black and white flag that ends a session. See [after the chequered flag](/guide/race/after-the-flag/).

### Clean air
Air that hasn't been disturbed by another car. A car in clean air has full downforce. The opposite of dirty air.

### Compound
The type of rubber in a tyre. Softer compounds are faster but wear out sooner. See [tyre compounds and colours](/guide/tyres/tyre-compounds/).

### Concorde Agreement
The contract between the FIA, Formula 1 and the teams covering governance and prize money. See [who runs F1](/guide/basics/who-runs-f1/).

### Constructor
The official name for a team, because it builds its own chassis. See [teams and drivers](/guide/basics/teams-and-drivers/).

### Constructors' Championship
The title for teams, decided by the combined points of both cars. See [the Constructors' Championship](/guide/points/constructors-championship/).

### Cool-down lap
The lap after the chequered flag, driven slowly back to the pits. Also called the in-lap.

### Corner mode
The high-downforce setting of the active wings, used in corners. See [active aerodynamics](/guide/car/active-aerodynamics/).

### Cost cap
The limit on how much a team can spend in a year. See [the cost cap](/guide/teams/cost-cap/).

## D

### Deg
Short for degradation: how much a tyre's performance drops as it is used. See [tyre wear and degradation](/guide/tyres/tyre-degradation/).

### Delta
A time difference. A driver might be told to stay above a "minimum delta" under the safety car, meaning a maximum speed set by the FIA.

### Dirty air
Turbulent air coming off the back of a car, which reduces the downforce of a car following closely. See [slipstream and dirty air](/guide/strategy/slipstream-and-dirty-air/).

### Dive-bomb
A late, ambitious lunge up the inside under braking.

### DNF
Did Not Finish. The driver retired from the race.

### DNQ
Did Not Qualify. Rare now, but possible under the [107% rule](/guide/qualifying/107-percent-rule/).

### DNS
Did Not Start. The driver qualified but didn't take the start.

### Downforce
Aerodynamic force pushing the car into the track, giving more grip in corners. See [downforce and aerodynamics](/guide/car/aerodynamics-basics/).

### Drag
Air resistance that slows the car down on straights.

### Drive-through penalty
The driver must drive through the pit lane at the speed limit without stopping. See [types of penalties](/guide/penalties/types-of-penalties/).

### Drivers' Championship
The title for drivers. See [the Drivers' Championship](/guide/points/drivers-championship/).

### DRS
Drag Reduction System. An opening rear wing flap used for overtaking from 2011 to 2025. Replaced in 2026 by active aero and overtake mode. See [what happened to DRS?](/guide/car/what-happened-to-drs/).

### DSQ
Disqualified. The driver's result is removed. See [disqualification](/guide/penalties/disqualification/).

### Dummy pit stop
When a team gets its pit crew ready to fool a rival into reacting, without the driver actually stopping.

## E

### ECU
Electronic Control Unit. Every car uses the same standard ECU, supplied for the FIA, which also displays flag signals to the driver.

### Energy recovery
Collecting energy under braking and storing it in the battery to use later. See [energy recovery and the battery](/guide/car/energy-recovery/).

### ERS
Energy Recovery System. The electrical part of the power unit: the motor-generator and the battery.

### Extreme wet
The full wet-weather tyre, with blue markings. See [intermediate and wet tyres](/guide/tyres/wet-weather-tyres/).

## F

### FIA
Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, the governing body that writes and enforces the rules. See [who runs F1](/guide/basics/who-runs-f1/).

### Flat spot
A worn patch on a tyre caused by locking a wheel under braking. It causes vibration and can force a pit stop.

### Floor
The underside of the car, which produces much of its downforce through ground effect.

### Flow-vis
Fluorescent paint used in practice to show how air flows over the car.

### Flying lap
A lap started at full speed, as opposed to an out-lap from the pits. Qualifying times are set on flying laps.

### Formation lap
The lap before the start, when drivers warm their tyres and brakes and then line up on the grid. See [the race start](/guide/race/race-start/).

### FP1, FP2, FP3
Free Practice 1, 2 and 3. See [practice sessions](/guide/basics/practice-sessions/).

## G

### Gap
Time behind the race leader on the timing screen. See [reading the timing screen](/guide/basics/reading-live-timing/).

### Graining
Small bits of rubber tearing off the tyre and sticking back on the surface, reducing grip until they wear away.

### Grand Prix
A round of the championship. French for "grand prize". Plural: Grands Prix.

### Grid penalty
A drop in starting position, often for using too many power unit parts. See [grid penalties](/guide/qualifying/grid-penalties/).

### Ground effect
Using the shape of the floor to create low pressure under the car, sucking it towards the track.

## H

### Halo
The titanium hoop over the cockpit that protects the driver's head. See [safety features on the car](/guide/car/safety-features/).

### HANS
Head and Neck Support. A collar worn by drivers that limits head movement in a crash. See [driver safety gear](/guide/officials/driver-safety-gear/).

### Hard
The hardest of the three dry compounds at an event, marked white. Slowest but most durable.

### Harvesting
Recovering energy into the battery, mainly under braking.

## I

### ICE
Internal Combustion Engine. The petrol engine part of the power unit.

### In-lap
The lap on which a driver comes into the pits.

### Intermediate
The tyre for a damp or drying track, with green markings. See [intermediate and wet tyres](/guide/tyres/wet-weather-tyres/).

### Interval
Time behind the car directly in front on the timing screen.

## J

### Jump start
Moving off the grid before the start lights go out. Detected by sensors and penalised. See [the race start](/guide/race/race-start/).

## K

### Kerb
The raised, painted strip at the edge of the track at corners.

### Kinetic energy
Energy of motion. The MGU-K recovers it under braking.

## L

### Lift and coast
Lifting off the throttle before the braking point and coasting, to save fuel, harvest energy or cool the brakes. See [tyre and energy management](/guide/strategy/managing-the-race/).

### Lock-up
When a wheel stops turning under braking, usually with a puff of tyre smoke. Can cause a flat spot.

### Lollipop
The sign on a pole once used to signal a driver to leave a pit stop. Now replaced by lights on the pit gantry.

## M

### Marshal
A trackside volunteer who waves flags, clears debris and recovers cars. See [marshals](/guide/officials/marshals/).

### Medium
The middle of the three dry compounds at an event, marked yellow.

### MGU-H
Motor Generator Unit, Heat. Recovered energy from the turbocharger. Removed from the power unit in 2026.

### MGU-K
Motor Generator Unit, Kinetic. The electric motor that recovers energy under braking and delivers power to the rear wheels. See [the power unit](/guide/car/power-unit/).

### Mini-sector
A small slice of the lap on live timing, shown as coloured bars.

### Monocoque
The carbon-fibre shell the driver sits in, also called the survival cell.

## N

### Neutralised
When racing is paused, under the safety car or virtual safety car.

## O

### Out-lap
The lap after leaving the pits, used to warm up the tyres.

### Overcut
Staying out longer than a rival, hoping to be faster on old tyres than they are on new ones as they warm up. See [the undercut and the overcut](/guide/strategy/undercut-and-overcut/).

### Oversteer
When the rear of the car slides more than the front. The car turns more sharply than the driver asked, and can spin.

### Overtake mode
Extra electrical energy available to a driver within one second of the car ahead. The main overtaking aid from 2026. See [overtake mode and boost](/guide/car/overtake-mode/).

## P

### Paddock
The area behind the garages where teams have their offices and hospitality.

### Parc fermé
French for "closed park". Rules that stop teams making major changes to the car between qualifying and the race. See [parc fermé](/guide/qualifying/parc-ferme/).

### Penalty points
Points added to a driver's super licence for driving offences. Twelve in 12 months means a race ban. See [penalty points and race bans](/guide/penalties/penalty-points/).

### Pit lane start
Starting the race from the end of the pit lane instead of the grid. See [starting from the pit lane](/guide/qualifying/pit-lane-starts/).

### Pit wall
The stand along the pit lane where team strategists and engineers sit during sessions.

### Plank
A strip under the car that is measured for wear after the race. Too much wear means disqualification. See [scrutineering and technical checks](/guide/car/scrutineering/).

### Pole position
First place on the starting grid, earned by the fastest qualifying lap.

### Porpoising
Bouncing at high speed, caused by the floor's downforce stalling and recovering over and over.

### Power unit (PU)
The full engine package: combustion engine, turbocharger, MGU-K, battery and control electronics. See [the power unit](/guide/car/power-unit/).

### Protest
A formal complaint by a team about another team's car or conduct. See [protests, appeals and the right of review](/guide/penalties/appeals/).

### Purple
On the timing screen, the fastest time by anyone in that session.

## Q

### Q1, Q2, Q3
The three knockout parts of qualifying. See [how qualifying works](/guide/qualifying/how-qualifying-works/).

### Quali
Short for qualifying.

## R

### Race control
The FIA office that runs each session. See [race control and the race director](/guide/officials/race-control/).

### Race director
The FIA official in charge of running the race. See [race control and the race director](/guide/officials/race-control/).

### Racing line
The fastest route around the track.

### Red flag
The session is stopped. See [red flags and suspended races](/guide/race/red-flags/).

### Reprimand
A formal warning from the stewards. Five in a season (at least four for driving) brings a grid penalty. See [types of penalties](/guide/penalties/types-of-penalties/).

### Reserve driver
A driver on standby to replace a race driver. See [rookies and reserve drivers](/guide/teams/rookies-and-reserves/).

### Right of review
A team's request for the stewards to look again at a decision because of new evidence. See [protests, appeals and the right of review](/guide/penalties/appeals/).

### Run-off
The area beyond the edge of a corner where a car can slow down if it goes off. See [circuit safety](/guide/officials/circuit-safety/).

## S

### Safety car (SC)
A road car that leads the field at reduced speed after an incident. See [the safety car](/guide/race/safety-car/).

### Scrutineering
The FIA's checks that a car meets the technical rules. See [scrutineering and technical checks](/guide/car/scrutineering/).

### Sector
One of three parts of a lap used for timing.

### Slick
A dry-weather tyre with no tread pattern.

### Slipstream
The pocket of low air resistance behind a car, which lets the car following go faster on a straight. Also called a tow. See [slipstream and dirty air](/guide/strategy/slipstream-and-dirty-air/).

### Soft
The softest of the three dry compounds at an event, marked red. Fastest but wears out quickest.

### Sprint
A short race of around 100 km held on Saturday at six events, with points for the top eight. See [how sprint weekends work](/guide/sprint/sprint-format/).

### Sprint Qualifying (SQ)
The session that sets the grid for the sprint. See [sprint qualifying](/guide/sprint/sprint-qualifying/).

### Stewards
The panel of officials that judges incidents and hands out penalties. See [the stewards](/guide/penalties/the-stewards/).

### Stint
The laps a driver completes on one set of tyres.

### Stop-go penalty
The driver must come into the pits and stop for 10 seconds, with no work allowed on the car. See [types of penalties](/guide/penalties/types-of-penalties/).

### Straight mode
The low-drag setting of the active wings, used on straights. See [active aerodynamics](/guide/car/active-aerodynamics/).

### Super licence
The FIA licence a driver needs to race in F1. See [the super licence](/guide/teams/super-licence/).

### Survival cell
The strong carbon-fibre tub around the driver, built to stay intact in a crash.

## T

### Team orders
Instructions from the team telling one driver to let the other past, or to hold position. See [team orders](/guide/strategy/team-orders/).

### Telemetry
Data sent from the car to the garage in real time: speed, temperatures, pressures and much more.

### Tow
Another word for slipstream.

### TPC
Testing of Previous Cars. Running an older car to give drivers mileage without breaking the testing ban. See [testing](/guide/teams/testing/).

### Track limits
The rule that drivers must keep the car within the white lines. See [track limits](/guide/penalties/track-limits/).

### Traction
Grip when accelerating, especially out of slow corners.

### Tyre blanket
An electric cover that heats tyres before they go on the car. The temperature they can reach is limited by the rules. See [tyre temperature and blankets](/guide/tyres/tyre-temperature/).

## U

### Undercut
Pitting before a rival and using the faster new tyres to get ahead when they stop. See [the undercut and the overcut](/guide/strategy/undercut-and-overcut/).

### Understeer
When the front of the car doesn't turn as much as the driver wants, and the car runs wide.

### Unsafe release
Sending a car out of its pit box into the path of another car. Penalised. See [pit stops and the pit lane](/guide/race/pit-stops/).

## V

### Virtual safety car (VSC)
Drivers must slow to a set speed everywhere on the track, without a safety car leading them. See [the virtual safety car](/guide/race/virtual-safety-car/).

## W

### Wet tyre
See extreme wet.

### Works team
A team that builds its own power unit or has an exclusive manufacturer partner. See [engine manufacturers and customer teams](/guide/teams/engine-suppliers/).

### World Motor Sport Council (WMSC)
The FIA body that gives final approval to rules and calendars. See [who runs F1](/guide/basics/who-runs-f1/).

## Y

### Yellow flag
Danger ahead: slow down, no overtaking. Double waved yellows mean be prepared to stop. See [yellow flags](/guide/flags/yellow-flags/).

## Numbers

### 107% rule
A driver whose qualifying time is more than 7% slower than the fastest Q1 time may not be allowed to start. See [the 107% rule](/guide/qualifying/107-percent-rule/).
