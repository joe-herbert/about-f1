Points are given to the top 10 finishers in every Grand Prix and the top eight in every sprint. The same points count twice: once for the driver, towards the [Drivers' Championship](/guide/points/drivers-championship/), and once for their team, towards the [Constructors' Championship](/guide/points/constructors-championship/).

## Grand Prix points

| Position | Points |
| --- | --- |
| 1st | 25 |
| 2nd | 18 |
| 3rd | 15 |
| 4th | 12 |
| 5th | 10 |
| 6th | 8 |
| 7th | 6 |
| 8th | 4 |
| 9th | 2 |
| 10th | 1 |

Finishing 11th or lower scores nothing. Those places still matter, though, because they're used to separate drivers and teams on equal points. See [how ties are broken](/guide/points/tie-breakers/).

## Sprint points

| Position | Points |
| --- | --- |
| 1st | 8 |
| 2nd | 7 |
| 3rd | 6 |
| 4th | 5 |
| 5th | 4 |
| 6th | 3 |
| 7th | 2 |
| 8th | 1 |

There are six [sprint weekends](/guide/sprint/sprint-format/) in 2026.

## The most you can score

| | Driver | Team (both cars) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal weekend | 25 | 43 (1st and 2nd) |
| Sprint weekend | 33 | 58 (1st and 2nd in both) |

## What you need to score

A driver has to be **classified** to score points. That means completing at least 90% of the laps the winner completed, rounded down. A driver who retires late in the race can still be classified, and score, if they'd already done enough laps. It's common to see a car parked by the side of the track listed in 10th.

If a driver is [disqualified](/guide/penalties/disqualification/), they lose any points from that result, and everyone behind moves up a place.

If two cars genuinely tie for a position, which is very unlikely with timing to the thousandth of a second, the points for the places they share are added together and split equally.

## Races that end early

Full points are only given if the leader completes at least 75% of the planned race distance. If a race is cut short, a reduced scale is used, and if fewer than two laps are run without a safety car, nobody scores at all. The scales are set out on the [shortened races](/guide/points/shortened-races/) page.

## No more fastest lap point

From 2019 to 2024, the driver who set the fastest lap of the race earned 1 extra point, as long as they finished in the top 10. It was dropped from 2025. The bonus had led to some odd tactics: drivers pitting near the end for fresh tyres just to chase it, and teams using a driver outside the top 10 to take the fastest lap away from a rival's title contender.

## How the system has changed

The points have changed many times since 1950. Some recent versions:

| Years | Points from 1st down |
| --- | --- |
| 1991 to 2002 | 10, 6, 4, 3, 2, 1 (top six) |
| 2003 to 2009 | 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (top eight) |
| 2010 onwards | 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1 (top ten) |

Because the scale has grown, modern drivers score many more points than older champions did. That makes comparing career points totals across eras misleading. Win counts or championships are a fairer comparison.

One season had a twist: in 2014, the final race in Abu Dhabi paid double points. It was widely disliked and only used once.

Until 1990, drivers could only count their best results from a set number of races and had to drop the rest. Since 1991, every result counts.
