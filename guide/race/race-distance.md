A Grand Prix is set at the smallest number of complete laps that adds up to more than 305 km. On a 5 km track that works out at 62 laps, and on a short track like the Red Bull Ring it's 71. Monaco is the exception: it runs to just over 260 km, because a full distance around its tight streets would take far too long.

Most races take between 80 and 100 minutes. If something slows things down, two time limits stop a race going on forever.

## The 2-hour limit

Racing can't go on for more than 2 hours from the start. If the 2 hours run out before the leader has finished the full distance, the leader takes the chequered flag at the end of the lap after the one in which the 2 hours ran out. That's done so the finish isn't sprung on the drivers mid-lap.

This is rare in a dry race, but it can happen when there are a lot of slow laps behind the [safety car](/guide/race/safety-car/) or [virtual safety car](/guide/race/virtual-safety-car/). The 2012 Singapore Grand Prix, a long night race with two safety car periods, hit the limit after 59 of its planned 61 laps.

## The 3-hour cap

If the race is stopped with a [red flag](/guide/race/red-flags/), the clock keeps running during the stoppage, but the time the race was stopped for is added on to the 2 hours. There's an overall cap, though: the race can't last more than 3 hours from the start, including all stoppages.

The cap used to be 4 hours. It was cut to 3 in 2022. The longest race in F1 history, the 2011 Canadian Grand Prix, lasted just over 4 hours because of a two-hour rain delay, which is one of the reasons the rules changed.

## Laps lost before the start

The race distance can also shrink before it begins:

- Each [aborted start](/guide/race/race-start/) or extra formation lap takes one lap off.
- If the race starts behind the safety car, every lap the safety car leads, minus one, comes off the total.

So a race scheduled for 58 laps that has an extra formation lap and then a start behind the safety car lasting three laps would be down to 57 and then to 55.

## If the flag comes out at the wrong time

The chequered flag is waved by a person, and people make mistakes. The rules cover both cases:

- If the flag is shown **too early**, the race is counted as finished when the leader last crossed the line before the flag. At the 2018 Canadian Grand Prix the flag was waved a lap early, so the official result was taken from the end of lap 68 instead of lap 70.
- If the flag is shown **too late**, the race is counted as finished when it should have finished.

## Which laps count

Laps run under the safety car and the virtual safety car count as race laps, even though nobody can race. That's why a long safety car period near the end can see a race finish with almost no green-flag running.

Whether a shortened race gives out full points depends on how far it got. That's covered in [points for shortened races](/guide/points/shortened-races/). Sprint races have their own shorter distance of about 100 km, which you can read about in [how sprint weekends work](/guide/sprint/sprint-format/).

## Who gets classified

To be classified in the results, a driver has to have completed at least 90% of the laps covered by the winner, rounded down. In a 57-lap race, that means 51 laps. A driver who retires late but has already done enough laps is still classified, and can still score points if they're high enough up the order. See [after the chequered flag](/guide/race/after-the-flag/) for more on how the result is decided.
